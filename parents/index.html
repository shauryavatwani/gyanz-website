For parents

Built for the families and the students.

Records stay on this computer.

Progress and records are kept on the student's own computer.

What leaves the computer.

What your child types to the tutor and writes as answers goes over the internet to the AI company the parent chooses, together with the lesson notes and a short summary of what the check found. The app also checks GitHub for updates.

No ads. No usage data.

Gyanz shows no ads and collects no usage data. It exists to help a student learn.

One step at a time.

Gyanz explains one idea, then asks the student to try a small step.

Questions? Answers.

Is it for CBSE?

Yes. For now, Gyanz is built only for the Indian CBSE curriculum. Every note and question is written from the current NCERT textbook, and checked against it.

Which classes and subjects?

Today: Class 10 Science, added chapter by chapter. Next: Class 10 Maths and Social Science, then Class 9.

Does it replace school or a teacher?

No. It helps a student practise and find what they missed, alongside school.

Does it need the internet?

Reading notes and answering multiple-choice questions work without it. The tutor and the marking of written answers need it.

Is my child's data shared?

Records stay on the computer. What your child types to the tutor and writes as answers goes over the internet to the AI company the parent chooses, together with the lesson notes and a short summary of what the check found.

That company may keep what it receives for up to 30 days, and longer in some cases. It is the parent's own account with them. Gyanz shows no ads and collects no usage data.

Is there a phone app?

Not now. Gyanz is an app for computers.

When can we use it?

Gyanz is in a pilot and is not released yet. Questions? Write to the1mteamdpsbe@gmail.com.
